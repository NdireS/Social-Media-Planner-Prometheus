# Thinking engines

Use at least six per run, and rotate them, so two runs never feel the same.
Each engine has a trigger question. Answer it for the specific business and push
past the first three answers (the first three are always generic).

| # | Engine | Trigger question |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Inversion** | What would the exact opposite of normal marketing in this category look like, and why might it work better? |
| 2 | **The missing decision** | What decision is every competitor avoiding because it feels risky, unprofitable or embarrassing? Make it. |
| 3 | **Time travel (+20 years)** | What will people in 2046 find absurd about how this service is sold today? Publish from that future. |
| 4 | **Time travel (−200 years)** | How was this need met before industry? What forgotten wisdom or ritual can come back? |
| 5 | **Cross-domain transplant** | Steal a mechanism from an unrelated field (aviation checklists, monastic rules, sports commentary, theatre, open-source software, beekeeping, jazz) and apply it here. |
| 6 | **Absurd constraint** | We may only post in one colour, one word, one location, in silence, or once a year. What would we make? Then relax the constraint by 10 %. |
| 7 | **Radical honesty** | What is true about this service that no one in the industry admits? Say it in public and build trust from it. |
| 8 | **Villain removal** | Who or what makes the customer's life harder (a system, a myth, a middleman)? Fight it publicly, not a competitor. |
| 9 | **The opposite customer** | Make content for the person who will *never* buy. What would make them share it anyway? |
| 10 | **Ritual invention** | Invent a repeatable ritual (weekly, seasonal or once in a lifetime) that the audience performs and owns. |
| 11 | **The thing you'd never say** | Write the sentence the legal department would cut, then find the version that is both true and allowed. |
| 12 | **Anti-content** | What if the post *removes* something from people's lives (noise, a task, a worry) rather than adding to the feed? |
| 13 | **Give away the crown jewel** | Publish the secret method openly. What does the business become when knowledge isn't the moat? |
| 14 | **Scale shift** | Zoom in to one molecule, one minute or one customer for a year. Zoom out to a whole city, a century or the planet. |
| 15 | **Participation flip** | The audience makes the brand's content and the brand becomes the audience. |
| 16 | **Slow in a fast feed** | Build one thing that unfolds over 30 or 100 days, where every post is a chapter. |
| 17 | **The unglamorous truth** | Film or write the boring, invisible 90 % of the work. Make that boring 90 % fascinating. |
| 18 | **Platform misuse** | Use a platform feature for something it was never designed for (Telegram polls as a story engine, LinkedIn documents as a comic, Bluesky custom feeds as a curriculum). |

## Thinker lenses (take the mechanism, never the surface)

Use one or two per run, from very different worlds. Examples:
- Systems and futures: Donella Meadows (leverage points), Stewart Brand (pace layers), Kevin Kelly (1,000 true fans, the inevitable).
- Philosophy: Seneca (negative visualisation), Ubuntu ("I am because we are"), Wabi-sabi (the beauty of the imperfect).
- Design: Dieter Rams (less but better), Christopher Alexander (pattern language).
- Science: Feynman (explain it to a child), Darwin (variation and selection, used as a content system).
- Art: Marina Abramović (endurance, presence), Banksy (unannounced public intervention), Ai Weiwei (accumulation of ordinary objects).
- Commerce outliers: Patagonia's "Don't buy this jacket", Basecamp's "It doesn't have to be crazy at work". These are **graveyard examples**: study why they were outliers in their time, then don't copy them.

## Generic-detector phrases (if these appear in an idea, reconsider it)

"tips", "hacks", "secrets revealed", "day in the life", "behind the scenes", "meet the team",
"myth vs fact", "POV", "did you know", "unpopular opinion", "here's why", "game changer",
"trend alert", "storytime", "we asked ChatGPT", "giveaway", "tag a friend", "Monday motivation".
